## P2 — Should be sourced soon

### "Lawns sequester negligible carbon"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 01; Factsheet § 02
- **Status:** True directionally — but "negligible" relative to what? Relative to the emissions from lawn equipment?
- **What we need:** Peer-reviewed comparison of lawn carbon sequestration vs. lawn maintenance emissions (mowers, fertilizer production). The balance is likely negative.
- **Candidates:** Milesi et al. (2005) touches on this; Townsend-Small & Czimczik (2010) "Carbon sequestration and greenhouse gas emissions in urban turf" — *Geophysical Research Letters*

### "Lawns support almost no insect life"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 01; Factsheet § 02; Zines 01 and 03
- **Status:** Asserted strongly — true in direction, but "almost no" needs qualification
- **What we need:** Peer-reviewed comparison of insect species supported by turf vs. native plantings. Doug Tallamy's work (*Bringing Nature Home*, 2007) is the classic citation here.
- **Candidates:** Tallamy, Doug W. & Shropshire, Karen J. (2009) "Ranking Lepidopteran use of native versus introduced plants" *Conservation Biology*; Narango et al. (2018) on birds and native plants

### "Compacted turf soil has significantly lower infiltration rates than diverse ground cover"
- **Where used:** Factsheet § 02 (stormwater claim)
- **Status:** Plausible engineering claim, no citation
- **What we need:** Soil science or hydrology literature on turf vs. native planting infiltration rates. Relevant to the stormwater/flooding argument.
- **Candidates:** Urban stormwater management literature; USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service publications

### "$153B landscaping industry — larger than the film industry"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 01 stat
- **Status:** The $153B is sourced (NALP/IBIS World). The "larger than film industry" comparison is unverified.
- **What we need:** US film industry revenue figure for same year. MPAA annual report or Box Office Mojo would have this. Also: are we comparing industry revenue to box office gross, or total film industry value? Apples-to-apples matters.
- **Note:** May be correct — US film box office is ~$8-9B/year; total film/TV production is ~$100-115B. At $153B total landscaping, the comparison likely holds for box office but may not hold for total industry. Verify and clarify the comparison.

### "Water rates up 24% in 5 years — faster than inflation"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 01 (new); Zine 02
- **Status:** Sourced to Bluefield Research, but Bluefield is a paid market research firm — their full methodology isn't publicly available
- **What we need:** A secondary confirmation from a government or academic source. EPA, GAO, or Congressional Budget Office may have published on water rate trends. ASCE infrastructure report card also covers this.
- **Search terms:** "water rate increases 2019 2024" site:epa.gov OR site:gao.gov

### "Buffalo, NY Thoreau scholar — $25,000 in fines"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 02; Factsheet § 03
- **Status:** Sourced to Pollan (1989) only — a 35-year-old essay describing a then-ongoing case
- **What we need:** Buffalo News archive search for this case. A law school clinic may have documented it. If cannot verify independently, must be explicitly attributed as "described by Pollan (1989)" — not stated as documented fact
- **Search terms:** Buffalo News archive: "wildflower" OR "lawn" "fine" 1985-1992; alternatively "Buffalo" "lawn ordinance" "Thoreau"

## P3 — Nice to have

### "Turf industry lobbyists understood that a mandate is better than a marketing campaign"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 02
- **Status:** Interpretive claim — reasonable inference but framed as historical fact
- **What we need:** Any documented history of turf/chemical industry lobbying for weed ordinance legislation. Virginia Scott Jenkins *The Lawn: A History of an American Obsession* (1994) may have this. Ted Steinberg *American Green* (2006) covers the political economy of the lawn.
- **Note:** This is an inferential claim about intent. Even if we can't source it directly, reframing as "ordinances that happen to serve turf industry interests" is defensible without the lobbyist assertion.

### "Frederick Law Olmsted designed the front lawn as continuous unfenced space"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 03; Factsheet § 04
- **Status:** Via Pollan (1989), not primary Olmsted sources
- **What we need:** Olmsted's own writing on Riverside (1868) or Brookline designs. Available via Library of Congress Olmsted Papers. Scott (1870) is already in bibliography but unverified against original text.
- **Note:** The Riverside, IL design is documented — the interpretation of intent (creating "appearance of collective space") is Pollan's reading, not Olmsted's stated intent. Worth flagging.

### "Several states have already preempted HOA restrictions on native planting"
- **Where used:** Manifesto § 05 demand 2; Factsheet § 05 alternatives

- **What we need:** A list of states with HOA preemption laws for native landscaping. Florida (HB 105, 2023) is documented. California, Texas, and others may have similar provisions.
- **Search terms:** "HOA preemption native plants state law" "right to landscape" legislation

- **Search terms:** "water affordability minimum wage" "hours worked water bill" "water burden low income"

## Methodology notes

**Forum quotes (Hatch, Lawn Forum, aroundtheyard, Bogleheads):** All used as illustrative testimony, flagged as `watch` in bibliography. They are real quotes from real forums but not independently verified. Acceptable for zines (where they function as testimony), not for factsheet claims.

**Industry self-reported data (CAI, NALP):** Used throughout. CAI figures benefit the HOA industry by showing scale/relevance. NALP figures benefit the landscaping industry. Both use credible underlying data (ACS, IBIS World) but the framing serves their interests. Note this in any policy-facing document.

**The "170×" comparison:** Only valid as US lawns vs. US data centers direct cooling water. Do not state without scope qualifiers. See factsheet § 06 and bibliography entry `construction-physics-datacenter-water-2025` for methodology details.
